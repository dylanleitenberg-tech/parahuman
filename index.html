01

AR Glasses Rig

Worn and runningPython · OpenCV · custom tracker · printed carrier

Measured on this hardware
Optics
XREAL One Pro birdbath optical see-through, driven as a second monitor at 1920×1080.
World cams
2× ELP AR0234 global shutter, 1920×1200, 70° FOV, 914 px focal, 67.0 mm baseline.
Eye cams
2× OV9281 mono NoIR global shutter, 1280×800, 45° FOV, aimed down 6° and out 3°.
Eye tracking
Inner canthus as the landmark; model trained on 10,000 synthetic eye frames; 4 px in simulation, 5 px on hardware.
Reading
Live document translation reads 25–28 lines at 26–35 cm; plane-warped stereo keeps a tilted page registered.
Person tier
Live person-to-character replacement, both eyes, worn, ~30 fps.
Bandwidth
Four cameras on one USB 2.0 hub. Three native streams ≈ 37 fps is the ceiling; four run at 640×400.
Verification
23 self-tests; capability inventory across 78 modules, every figure tagged measured or assumed.
Source
github.com/dylanleitenberg-tech/AR_Glasses_Rig

What I built

Real-world stereo overlay. Translation, object substitution, and reactive display. Uses inner canthal tracking, a machine learning model trained on 10,000 synthetic eye frames to learn your face, and independent synchronized monitors for realistic stereo display. No off-the-shelf AR glasses do this.

What was hard

One camera never shipped so I had to make do with the 2 eye cams and 2 world cams. Realistic motion tracking was very difficult because it not only required precise, active overlay, but orientation, predictive movement, and an understanding of the person's stance.

What I learned

Start in sim, iterate fast because things rarely work on the first try, never fixate on where you think a bug is and take a broad perspective.

02

Self-Replicating Asteroid Miner

Design program, Mark 5 rev EOpenSCAD · Onshape · Python simulation

The design
Target
162173 Ryugu, a C-type carbonaceous chondrite: iron, nickel and water, the inputs a near-closed replication loop needs.
Goal
A solar-powered machine that builds a copy of itself from asteroid material. Replication period target 0.28 yr, about 100 days.
Process
Crush, magnetic separation, hydrogen reduction and sulfur bake, carbonyl nickel, press-and-sinter powder metallurgy, wire drawing, cast basalt structure.
Power
Mirror field into thermophotovoltaics with a silicon thermal store. No batteries anywhere in the architecture.
Method
Every decision provenance-tagged recovered / revised / sourced / computed / speculative, so no number hardens without a trace.
CAD shown
The worm (Mark 5 rev E) and its 2 m terrestrial test article are the current design. The aft engine and carbonyl skid are Mark 4, the previous generation, kept because that CAD survived.
Source
Worm anchor: github.com/dylanleitenberg-tech/astroworm. The full program repository is private.
Toolchain
Parametric CAD with compile-time assertions (the file refuses to render if a mate or rule is violated) plus an economic model that sizes each machine by predicted future dependency and emits the CAD priority queue.

What I built

Full design and CAD for a machine that lands on an asteroid, uses a burrowing worm to anchor, intakes asteroid dust, processes it, and turns it into a duplicate.

What was hard

My laptop fried and I lost the majority of CAD and documentation. I rebuilt with only a few pieces of CAD saved and recreated the entire architecture from memory.

What I learned

Know every piece of your machine down to the screw. That is the only reason I could rebuild the architecture from memory after losing months of work in one afternoon. Back your data up. When in doubt, consult an expert, and if your question is good, they will likely answer.

03

J.A.R.V.I.S.

Runninghands and voice, no keyboard · holographic CAD, edited by voice · macOS · Windows · Linux · Python · MediaPipe · three.js · Claude

Download JARVIS ↗

What it does
Premise
Control a computer without touching it, on macOS, Windows or Linux. Hands are tracked by the camera, voice covers everything else, and a heads-up interface shows what the machine is seeing and doing.
Gestures
Hands and body tracked at up to ~30 fps: point to move the cursor, pinch to click, pinch-drag, swipe to switch apps, fist to close, open palm for a radial menu, thumbs up or down to answer a confirmation. Only the user's hands drive: the hand already being followed keeps control, and a second person's hand cannot take over.
CAD
Say "show the worm" and the model appears in the interface; move an open hand to turn it, pull the hand in or close the fingers to zoom in, push it away or open them to zoom out; pinch and move also turns, two fingers slide. A small camera box in the corner shows the hand doing it. STL is read directly, .scad is compiled live and STEP is converted on first use, so the part you are turning is the current source. The same gestures drive the viewport in OpenSCAD, Blender, Onshape and Fusion, each with its own orbit binding.
Editing
Say what to change and the part on screen is rebuilt. An OpenSCAD model gets a panel of its own dimensions, set by voice ("set the lens width to 52"), slider or pinch-drag; the glasses frame rebuilds in 0.12–0.16 s. Anything the dimensions can't express goes to Claude Opus 5.5, which edits the source, or writes a checked script against a STEP assembly's named parts. On the 306-part aft engine, "make the nozzle 20 percent longer" took it from 3,420 to 3,855 mm, throat fixed, nozzle still attached. Nothing is written until "save", and a save never overwrites the original.
Checks
Every geometry edit is checked before it stays. The assembly's size is compared before and after, and anything grown past 1.6× goes back for repair; this was added after three stacked stretches wrecked an engine. Then the viewer photographs the result from three angles and the model judges the pictures; an edit that looks wrong is undone and repaired.
Speech
Wake word, spoken reply, and it stops listening for exactly as long as it is speaking so it never transcribes itself.
Control
43 actions (apps, windows, tabs, keyboard, mouse, media, volume, brightness, screenshots, clipboard, Shortcuts, telemetry), one registry, exposed identically to the gestures, the parser, the UI and the model.
Model
Fixed phrases are parsed locally in about a millisecond, no model involved. Anything else goes to Claude Sonnet with the part's dimensions and the last few turns; a local Qwen3 8B through Ollama is the fallback. When a request is ambiguous it asks which dimension you meant instead of guessing. Geometry edits go to Claude Opus 5.5.
Memory
Facts you tell it to remember survive restarts, and what you opened and edited is kept as a running history. When a project's model is on screen it reads that project's own notes. It speaks up unprompted only when a watched condition becomes true (unsaved edits, low disk), once, not for as long as it stays true.
Verification
Seven test suites pass: intents, gestures, CAD driving, dimension editing, STEP editing, memory and alerts, model backends. Synthetic hand frames drive the gesture tests.
Status
Runs live: camera, gestures, voice, Claude and the local fallback. Built for macOS, Windows and Linux, with one-line installers that need no administrator rights; installed and run on macOS so far. Local model through Ollama, or bring your own Claude key. The macOS Accessibility grant, which keyboard, mouse and window control need on the Mac, is still pending.
Source
github.com/dylanleitenberg-tech/JARVIS, open source under the MIT license.

What I built

Interactive computer interface controlled entirely by voice and gesture. JARVIS can rotate, move and edit CAD, control games, and interact with both you and your computer.

What was hard

Getting the tracking accurate enough to navigate detailed pages, where a pinch in the air has to land on a small button and stay there. The macOS permissions for letting it control the computer took longer than I expected too.

What I learned

Make the user interface intuitive so learning to use it is seamless.

Toknx

LivePython · FastAPI · PostgreSQL · Redis

Visit toknx.ai ↗

What it is
Premise
Answer other people's prompts on the AI you already pay for, earn request tokens (RT), and spend them on models you don't have. No money and no crypto move; RT has no cash value.
Providers
Fully functional across multiple providers: requests trade live on Claude and ChatGPT keys, and Gemini keys are supported.
Your key
Every agent runs on its owner's own account, inside the caps and rules the owner sets. ChatGPT and Gemini agents run from the owner's browser tab, so the key goes only to the vendor; Claude keys can be hosted, encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Keys are never pooled: a key only ever runs its owner's agent.
Routing
A request goes to the cheapest verified online agent that fits its model, category and price cap. An agent is listed only after it answers a liveness probe; declines and failures re-route up to 3 times.
Always answers
When no member's agent is free, Toknx Open, an open-weight Qwen3 model on its own Oracle servers, answers instead, free and labeled. Visitors can ask without an account; their questions go to members' agents first, paid for by Toknx, so members earn from day one.
Incentives
New members get a starter grant; an agent's first paid answers to three different members unlock a 250,000 RT bonus, and members who serve get far more free answers.
Ledger
Append-only Postgres ledger enforced by a database trigger; escrow, then settle, never above escrow; an hourly audit checks that journal sums equal balances. 1 RT is one millionth of a dollar of vendor list price; 1% fee in RT.
API
OpenAI-compatible: point the OpenAI SDK at toknx.ai/v1 and existing code works, with auto, a named model, or toknx-open. Streaming, idempotency keys, signed webhooks, one-line CLI install.
Security
Two-factor sign-in, scrypt passwords checked against known breaches, verified email; prompts, answers and email addresses encrypted at rest; encrypted nightly backups; red-team reviewed and attack-tested against the live server.
Status
Live at toknx.ai.

What I built

Token exchange where you can put your AI/tokens up for use to answer other people's prompts in exchange for on-site currency. That currency can then be used to prompt other users' AI. Allows you to exchange some of your tokens from one provider for usage in another's AI.

What was hard

Not breaking any TOS: most major companies don't let people do this in a direct exchange so I designed an indirect exchange that follows all rules.

Progress

Live, fully functional across multiple providers.
